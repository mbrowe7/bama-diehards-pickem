## 1. The Six League Quirks That Actually Change Strategy

Most generic dynasty advice assumes a standard 1-QB, infinite-timeline, taxi-is-bonus-space league. Yours isn't that. Here's what's actually different and what it means:

| Rule | What it means for your draft |
|---|---|
| **Superflex (WRTQ slot)** | You need 2 startable QBs, not 1. QB is the scarcest, longest-lived, highest-ceiling asset in your format. This is the single biggest lever in the whole draft. |
| **0.5 PPR, no TE premium** | Volume WRs and pass-catching RBs are king. Don't overpay for TE just because Bowers/McBride are good players — there's no scoring bonus propping up the position. |
| **No DST slot at all** | Despite the scoring page showing defense categories, you confirmed there's no roster spot for it. **Never draft a defense.** Ignore that scoring section entirely — it's leftover platform template, not usable. |
| **K start-1, scores per FG yard** | Mildly non-standard (long kicks matter a bit more), but not enough to spend real capital on. Last round, period. |
| **23 total roster spots = 10 start + 10 bench + 3 taxi** | This is the sneaky one. In a lot of dynasty leagues, taxi squads are *bonus* space on top of your bench. **Here, taxi is carved out of your 23, not added to it.** Every rookie you stash — taxied or not — costs you one of only 23 total assets. You can't hoard rookies "for free." Draft picks/dart-throws have a real opportunity cost against roster-building veterans. |
| **Title = 3 straight or 4 total, then the whole league re-drafts from scratch** | This isn't an infinite game. If *anyone* in the league hits the threshold, everyone's roster gets wiped — including yours. That's actually good news for a "balanced" approach: a slow build isn't a life sentence, because the league has a built-in reset valve regardless of what you do. It also means you shouldn't overpay in future 1sts for a marginal "prove it now" veteran — patience is structurally protected here in a way it isn't in a truly permanent dynasty league. |

**Rookie draft order nuance worth knowing for trade valuation:** playoff teams' rookie picks (slots 7–12) are fully determined by finish — no randomness. But the 6 non-playoff picks are set by a lottery ("duck race") weighted by inverse max PF. That means a 2027 1st-round pick you acquire from a bad non-playoff team is *not* a guaranteed 1.01 — it's a lottery ticket, more like an NBA draft pick than a locked-in slot. Discount those picks ~10–15% versus a known playoff-team pick when trading.

## 2. The Core Algorithm

Every pick, run this in order. Don't skip steps.

**Step 1 — Positional Scarcity Multiplier.**
In superflex, there are only ~24 QBs worth rostering (2 per team × 12) before you're starting replacement-level guys who actively lose you games. RB and WR have much deeper benches league-wide. Practically: treat a "top-12 dynasty QB" as roughly equivalent in trade value to a "top-4 dynasty RB/WR" — the cliff is that steep. Weight QB up accordingly at every pick where the talent gap to the next player is small.

**Step 2 — Age/Timeline Adjustment.**
- RBs: value cliffs hard after 26–27. A 23-year-old RB1 is worth meaningfully more than a 27-year-old RB1 with identical current production.
- WRs: peak later, decline slower — 24–29 is usually the prime window, so a 26-year-old proven WR1 is still a great "balanced" asset, not just a win-now rental.
- QBs: longest shelf life by far. A locked-in 25-year-old franchise QB can be a 10-year asset.
- TEs: often break out late (age 25–27), so don't punt a good young TE just because he hasn't hit yet.

**Step 3 — Roster Fit / Timeline Bucket.**
Since you're going balanced, every pick should answer one of two questions:
1. "Does this make me competitive in the next 1–2 seasons?" (early rounds, proven production)
2. "Is this a 22-or-under asset who could be a league-winner in year 2–4?" (mid-to-late rounds, upside)
Avoid the trap zone in between: 27+ year old players who are neither foundational long-term pieces nor true difference-makers right now. That's where reaches happen.

**Step 4 — Take the highest (Scarcity × Timeline-adjusted value) player left, then check roster fit last**, not first. Best-player-available wins startups; positional need-drafting in the 23-round startup is a beginner mistake because the depth of the draft means you'll fill needs anyway.

## 5. Pick 1.05 Decision Tree

You said you expect the room to reach QB-heavy early. Use that:

- **If an elite RB (Bijan/Gibbs-tier) is somehow still on the board at 1.05** because the room went QB-crazy on picks 1–4 → take him immediately. RBs age out fastest, so getting one at a discount because others panic-drafted QBs is the best kind of value in this format.
- **If the board falls "chalk"** (best players go roughly in order) → your realistic 1.05 options are Jaxon Smith-Njigba, Drake Maye, or Puka Nacua. Between an elite ascending WR and a young franchise QB1 at the exact same tier, lean **QB** given the 14-pick gap to your next selection and superflex scarcity — unless you're confident (based on how your specific league mocks/talks) that a Tier-4/5 QB (Caleb Williams, Lamar Jackson, Jayden Daniels) will still be sitting at pick 20.
- **Either way**, treat rounds 1–2 as "lock two long-term cornerstones," not "win 2026." A balanced build wants foundational pieces first, then layers in win-now depth from round 4 onward.

## 6. Round-by-Round Game Plan

- **Rounds 1–3:** Best player available by the algorithm. Target: 1 franchise QB + 1 true RB1/WR1 caliber player + one more top-30 asset. Don't draft a rookie here unless it's a true consensus 1.01-caliber rookie — the discount for waiting on rookies until their landing spot/role clarifies is real.
- **Rounds 4–8:** Fill your starting 10. Mix proven WR2/RB2/TE1 production with ascending 2nd/3rd-year breakout candidates. This is also where this year's rookie class studs typically get drafted once ADP has settled.
- **Rounds 9–15:** Depth and youth. Handcuff/change-of-pace RBs in good offenses, rookie fliers, backup QBs who are one injury from relevance (extra valuable in superflex). This is your taxi-squad hunting ground — remember, taxi eligibility only covers rookies/2nd-years, so use your 3 taxi spots on your highest-variance youth, not proven vets.
- **Rounds 16–22:** Pure lottery tickets — buried rookies, injury-recovery projects, backup TEs. Low cost, real if rare upside.
- **Round 23 (last pick):** Kicker. Don't think about it before this.

## 7. Post-Draft Philosophy (Balanced Build)

- Re-evaluate your realistic contention window after year 1. If you're closer than expected, sell some future rookie picks (especially lottery-weighted non-playoff-team picks, which are worth less than they look) for a proven vet. If you're further, do the reverse.
- Because a league-wide reset is baked into the rules, don't be afraid to be patient in years where you're not close — there's a structural floor under "How long do I have to be bad?" that most dynasty leagues don't have.
- Revisit this board the night before your draft — dynasty ADP moves fast, especially around this time of year with camp/preseason news.
